L-SR1: Learned Symmetric-Rank-One Preconditioning

Gal Lifshitz, Shahar Zuler, Ori Fouks, Dan Raviv
Tel Aviv University, Tel-Aviv, Israel
International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026
arXiv PDF Code

Abstract

End-to-end deep learning has achieved impressive results but remains limited by its reliance on large labeled datasets, poor generalization to unseen scenarios, and growing computational demands. In contrast, classical optimization methods are data-efficient and lightweight but often suffer from slow convergence. While learned optimizers offer a promising fusion of both worlds, most focus on first-order methods, leaving learned second-order approaches largely unexplored.

We propose a novel learned second-order optimizer that introduces a trainable preconditioning unit to enhance the classical Symmetric-Rank-One (SR1) algorithm. This unit generates data-driven vectors used to construct positive semi-definite rank-one matrices, aligned with the secant constraint via a learned projection. Our method is evaluated through analytic experiments and on the real-world task of Monocular Human Mesh Recovery (HMR), where it outperforms existing learned optimization-based approaches. Featuring a lightweight model and requiring no annotated data or fine-tuning, our approach offers strong generalization and is well-suited for integration into broader optimization-based frameworks.

Motivation

L-SR1 is a learned quasi-Newton optimizer that uses compact, learned modules to build a rank-one curvature preconditioner from limited memory.

Key challenge. SR1-style curvature can be indefinite, which leads to unstable descent directions.

Our fix: PGSM. PGSM (Projection-Guided Secant Mechanism) enforces a PSD rank-one preconditioner while guiding it with a secant penalty during meta-training.

Core question. Can learned optimizers benefit from curvature information? We show that curvature-aware updates lead to faster convergence, improved stability, and stronger generalization across dimensions and tasks.

Background

Learned optimization. Replace the hand-designed update with a learned map xk+1 = xk + φΘ(∇f(xk), xk, …), where Θ is shared across steps. Meta-training unrolls for K steps and minimizes ℒmeta = (1/K) Σk f(xk).

SR1 & quasi-Newton methods. Update with preconditioner Bk ≈ inverse Hessian: xk+1 ← xk − αk Bk gk. Secant condition (pk = xk − xk−1, qk = gk − gk−1): Bk qk = pk. SR1: Bk ← Bk−1 + uk vk⊤. Catch: SR1 need not be PSD ⇒ unstable steps; PSD ⇒ descent directions.

Learned SR1 (L-SR1)

State. Limited-memory buffer ℬL storing rank-one vectors; learned modules: encoder ℰ, vector generator 𝒫, LR generator 𝒢.

History. ℋk−1 = (xk−1, pk−1, dk−1, gk−1, qk−1): previous iterate, step, descent direction, gradient, and gradient difference.

One iteration k (given xk−1, buffer ℬL):

  1. Compute gradient gk−1 ← ∇f(xk−1).
  2. Encode features hk−1 ← ℰ(ℋk−1).
  3. Generate rank-one vector vk ← 𝒫(hk−1); update memory ℬL ← push(ℬL, vk).
  4. Form PSD preconditioner B̃k ← I + Σv∈ℬL vv⊤.
  5. Direction dk ← B̃k gk−1.
  6. Step sizes αk ← 𝒢(hk−1).
  7. Update xk ← xk−1 − αk ⊙ dk.

PGSM: PSD + secant guidance

PGSM = Projection-Guided Secant Mechanism.

Why PGSM? SR1-style updates can be indefinite ⇒ unstable descent directions. Enforcing PSD via vv⊤ is stable but risks violating the secant relation.

PSD preconditioner (rank-one sum):

B̃k = I + Σi=1L vivi⊤, with vi from the learned generator.

Meta-objective with secant penalty:

ℒmeta = (1/K) Σk ( f(xk) + λsec ‖pk − B̃kqk‖² ).

PGSM encourages stable PSD curvature while respecting secant information through the meta-objective—at no extra cost at inference time.

Analytic experiments

Quadratic functions. Train on random quadratics; test on higher-dimensional ones. L-SR1 generalizes without retraining. PGSM aligns updates with the Newton direction and outperforms the no-projection variant on unseen dimensions.

Performance profiles. Compared vs. no-PGSM, L-BFGS, Adam, and AdaHessian on a benchmark suite (quadratic, Rosenbrock, and Rastrigin objectives across varying dimension). L-SR1 with PGSM achieves the best overall performance profile.

Human mesh recovery (3DPW)

Task. Recover a 3D human mesh from a single RGB image. Setup. AMASS-trained; evaluated on 3DPW without fine-tuning.
